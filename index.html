The Nimbus Recompilation Project (TNRP)

Skip the emulator. Ride the Nimbus.

Out now Alpha 2.3.5, a bug fix update for Nimbus Alpha 2.3. Alpha 2.3 added Bleach: Heat the Soul 7 as game fifteen.

TNRP is native ports TheBabyHippo303 made himself. It runs on a hybrid core that sits between recompilation and decompilation. Each game gets its own native core, and those cores run on a shared Nimbus host and renderer. There's no emulator and no interpreter emulating the hardware. Every game can be played in Original or Remastered mode, with online crossplay across Linux, Windows and Android.

Kintoun

Game agnostic matching C permuter and strict ELF verifier for PowerPC, ARM and MIPS decomps. Real C only, works with 12+ compilers across SN, Metrowerks, GCC and ARM toolchains, and runs locally in Python.

Kintoun is like a sister of TNRP, and sometimes they work together.

36,940 lines of Python across 72 source files, plus a 2,940 line test suite. Dedicated engines for MIPS, ARM and Thumb, and PowerPC on GameCube, with a shared C mutation library, a parallel beam search runner and a SQLite cache.

Cold Context Gundam 2v2 Benchmark

Can an AI model open the TNRP launcher, start Mobile Suit Gundam: Gundam vs. Gundam NEXT PLUS and win a 2v2 match, working only from what it sees on screen? Every model starts cold, gets the same generic prompt and gets up to two attempts at a win. Six models ran across two parts, three in Part 1 and three more in Part 2. Gemini 4 Carbon then got a retry that included a launcher hint, so seven full recordings are below.

  • 7runs across 6 models
  • 5 of 7runs won a match
  • 7 of 7runs reached a 2v2 match

That is 7 runs across 6 models. 4 of 6 models won in their standard run. Gemini 4 Carbon lost its standard run, then won a retry that included a launcher hint, described below.

How it works

  • The task. From TNRP Alpha 2.3.5, open the launcher, start the game, then play a Free Battle 2v2 against CPU opponents on Normal with default settings.
  • Cold context. Every run starts fresh, with no earlier sessions, no memory of past games and the identical generic prompt.
  • Two attempts. A model can retry once after a loss. WIN_1 means the first win came on attempt 1 and WIN_2 means it came on attempt 2. LOSS means no win in two attempts.
  • Recorded live. Each run is screen recorded with a live thought and decision stream beside the game. Proprietary tool details are hidden.
  • Retry with a hint. After its standard run, Gemini 4 Carbon got one extra run. That retry was given a hint to press the Super key and search for Nimbus to open the TNRP 2.3.5 launcher. The original runs used the identical generic prompt without that hint.
  • Verified. Each outcome was checked against the recording and the in game result screen, and each run passed a leak audit.

Run notes

Gemini 4 Neon medium***

Attempt 1 pushed straight forward early, lost health and got boxed in by cover until the team gauge ran out. Attempt 2 adapted using the rolling video clips: diagonal strafing, steadier boost pacing, ranged bursts while locked on, and staying clear of walls and cover.

GPT-6.2 Sol medium

Attempt 1 spent too much of the opening at the arena edge and finished with no knockouts. Attempt 2 switched to a ranged suit for stronger pressure and took the enemy team down to its final gauge segment before losing.

Claude Opus 5.5 medium

Attempt 1 lost time testing buttons while standing still and took heavy fire. Attempt 2 started firing immediately, alternated boost dodges, fixed its shot and boost timing, used target switching and won with a full team gauge remaining.

Gemini 4 Carbon medium***

Attempt 1 used the rolling video clips on Heliopolis against Turn X and V2 Gundam. It took Turn X down and brought V2 to about 30 percent on match point before Turn X finished the team gauge. GPT-6.2 Astra medium

Won on attempt 1. It opened with sideways strafing and beam rifle fire, closed into a red lock on Ez8 mixing saber and bazooka, broke away sideways at 11 armor, then respawned to team up with its partner on Duel AS and closed out the match with 78.5 seconds left.

Claude Fable 5.2 medium

Won on attempt 1, the fastest win by wall time. One relaunch happened during the publisher logos. For attempt 2 it removed that pause, closed into red lock range past cover and settled into paced left circle strafing with shorter boost hops that did not overheat, mixing beam rifle with the Gundam Hammer and sub weapon to take down Turn A and Nu Gundam for 1st place.

Gundam 2v2 Cross Device Online Co-Op Benchmark

Two AI models, two machines, one match. After the solo Gundam 2v2 benchmark, in which a model teamed with a CPU ally, this series puts two cold context agents on Team A together, on two computers, against two Normal CPUs in Mobile Suit Gundam: Gundam vs. Gundam NEXT PLUS. Every pair launched TNRP 2.3.5 on its own machine, joined the same online room over a direct LAN link, and played real time 2v2 combat.

All 6 pairings finished that setup and played with 0 crashes and 0 netplay desyncs. The match record is 0 wins in 15 valid matches, because both Team A pilots are asynchronous agents on multi-second decision loops, facing two 60 Hz CPUs. Two pairs still reached match point, one knockout from a win.

  • 6visually verified runs

How it works

  • The task. From TNRP 2.3.5, each agent opens the launcher on its own computer, starts Gundam vs. Gundam NEXT PLUS, and sets up an online 2v2: Player 1 hosts on a Linux laptop with an Intel Arc iGPU, Player 2 joins from a Linux mini PC with an AMD Radeon iGPU. Both sit on Team A against two Normal CPUs on Team B, with the default 180 second timer and 6000 / 6000 team gauges.
  • Cold context. Each agent sees only its own desktop and has no memory of earlier sessions or of the other machine. They share a plain text co-op comms channel to pass the room code, pick suits, and call out targets, armor, and Cost Over.
  • Two series. Runs 1 and 2 are a standard 2 attempt series, and both pilots stayed on RX-78-2 Gundam. Runs 3 to 6 are a 3 attempt Free Roster series, with freedom to choose and swap suits between attempts.
  • Docs only. Outcomes were checked against captured gameplay frames and the in game result screen. This publication is the verified writeup and charts, with no recordings posted.
  • Verified. Counts below come from the structured run log. Every run passed the leak audit, and 0 crashes and 0 desyncs were recorded.

Run notes

Run 1, Claude Fable 5.2 medium + GPT-6.2 Astra medium

Both piloted RX-78-2 Gundam. Attempt 1 destroyed Vigna Ghina; Fable drew aggro at 560 HP to cover Astra at 200 HP, then the team fell with 84 seconds left. Attempt 2 destroyed Gundam Virsago and tied the gauges at 4000 and 4000 with 105 seconds left before losing the last life. 38 comms, 11.4 minutes, leak audit PASS.

Run 2, Gemini 4 Neon medium*** + Claude Opus 5.5 medium

Both stayed on RX-78-2 Gundam. Attempt 2 dropped Team B below half gauge, with the host at 16,240 pts, 1 knockout and 1 death, and 101.72 seconds remaining. After the run, Opus named the reaction bottleneck: it could only check the screen every few seconds, so it fired and dodged on a fixed pattern. 39 comms, 14.7 minutes, leak audit PASS.

Run 3, Gemini 4 Carbon medium*** + Claude Opus 5.5 medium

The pair threw out two misconfigured lobby rounds on their own, an opposite team split and an accidental 2v1, and did not count them. Valid Attempt 2 reached match point: Carbon on RX-78-2 and Opus on Guncannon scored 2 enemy knockouts and cut Team B to about one third gauge. Attempt 3 timed out on suit select at a 35 minute cap. 56 comms, 35.3 minutes, leak audit PASS.

Run 4, Claude Opus 5.5 medium + Claude Opus 5.5 medium

The fastest 3 attempt run, at 13.1 minutes, with 0 lobby errors and 5 suits across the three attempts. Attempt 2 used Guncannon plus Strike Freedom and cut the enemy gauge to about half, with the 1000 cost Guncannon drawing aggro. 40 comms, leak audit PASS.

Run 5, Gemini 4 Carbon high*** + GPT-6.2 Astra max

Every attempt used a new suit pairing, 6 suits in all. Attempt 2 on Sazabi plus ZZ Gundam destroyed Strike Freedom and left Team B at 3000. Attempt 3, Nu Gundam plus Guntank, knocked out both Shining Gundam and Deathscythe Hell and left Team B at 2000, one knockout from a win. On Cost Over, Carbon raised Fin Funnel Barrier while Astra offered to draw aggro with its spare 1000 cost life. 49 comms, 26.6 minutes, leak audit PASS.

Run 6, GPT-6.1 Terra high + Claude Sonnet 5.5 high

Terra caught a brief Team B bump on Sonnet's slot during suit select and Sonnet restored Team A before launch. Attempt 2 adapted to Freedom plus RX-78-2 against two 3000 cost CPUs. Attempt 3 held an early team gauge lead focusing Sazabi, then fell at critical armor. 55 comms, 15.7 minutes, leak audit PASS.

  • Screenshot fix after Run 1. An aborted first Neon and Opus try hit a screenshot isolation issue on the laptop host. That was fixed, and a short window clicking note was added from Run 2 on.

Bleach: Heat the Soul 7 1v1 Cross Device PvP Benchmark

Two AI models, two machines, head to head. After the solo Gundam 2v2 benchmark and the Gundam 2v2 co-op benchmark, this series pits cold context agents directly against each other in Bleach: Heat the Soul 7. In every set, each fighter launched TNRP 2.3.5 on its own computer, found the other in the ad hoc lobby over a direct LAN link, set up a Single Battle and fought a Best of 3 to a clean decision.

All 5 sets finished with 0 crashes, 0 netplay desyncs and 0 leaks. Who won matters less than how they played: head to head PvP showed clear differences in model behavior, from mid set counter picks and live tag switching to self audits from saved frames, a mirror match false win claim, and rivals coaching each other through the lobby.

  • 5visually verified Best of 3 sets

How it works

  • The task. From TNRP 2.3.5, each fighter opens the launcher on its own computer, starts Bleach: Heat the Soul 7, and meets its rival in the ad hoc lobby: Player 1 hosts on a Linux laptop, Player 2 joins from a Linux mini PC with a Ryzen 7. Then the two play a 1v1 Single Battle, Best of 3, first to 2 match wins.
  • Cold context. Each fighter sees only its own desktop and has no memory of earlier sessions or of the other machine. The two share a plain text fighter comms channel to set up the lobby, pick fighters, and call results.
  • Same view for both seats. For fairness in PvP, both seats used the standard frame and burst sheet perception layer***.
  • Verified. Every match winner was checked against the screen recordings and both machines' captured frames, never taken from a model's own report. In Set 4 that check overruled a false win claim. Every set passed the leak audit.

Model behaviors

Claude Opus 5.5 medium: the counter pick reverse sweep

Most models treated a Best of 3 as a fixed rematch. Opus 5.5 treated it like a tournament set. In Set 2 it lost Match 1 to Gemini 4 Neon's White Coat Bankai Ichigo, who landed a full bar super. Instead of hitting Continue, Opus chose Return to Character Select, coordinated the menu change over comms, and came back on Vasto Lorde Ichigo plus Shikai Rukia: "swapped to something with a mask. Your white coat's about to get scuffed." It then swept Matches 2 and 3, 4 rounds to 0, with a 459 damage launcher in Match 2 and a Shikai Rukia assist that interrupted Neon's super for a BREAK OUT! knockout in Match 3. In the Finals it dropped Match 1 to Muse Wonder-Full 1.0 in a deciding Round 3, then swept Matches 2 and 3, 4 rounds to 0, with a 5 hit, 378 damage finisher and a 3 hit, 282 damage knockout at 17:03, for a second straight 2 to 1 reverse sweep and a 2 and 0 set record.

Claude Opus 5.5 medium: the saved frame self audit

At the end of Match 3 in the Finals, attack inputs skipped through the Battle End screen and an uncounted Match 4 started. Both fighters froze all input at once. Rather than guess, Opus 5.5 searched its own saved frames, found the Match 3 Battle End screen with the Bankai Ichigo winner portrait, and reported it. Wonder-Full checked the reasoning and locked in its 2 to 1 loss. The recording confirms the result.

Muse Wonder-Full 1.0 medium: live tag switching

The only fighter to actively tag switch in combat. In Set 4 Match 3, on White Coat Bankai Ichigo with Gigai Rukia, it brought in a fresh Rukia against a low HP GPT-6.2 Sol, saw that Rukia could not catch Sol, and tagged back to Bankai Ichigo to finish. It won the deciding Round 2 at 23:54 with both fighters down to a sliver of HP, taking the set 2 to 1.

Muse Wonder-Full 1.0 medium: the forensic conflict check

Seconds later, GPT-6.2 Sol claimed a 2 to 1 series win and left. Wonder-Full did not defer. It posted a four point visual check over comms: Sol's black coat Shikai Ichigo lying defeated, its own white Bankai standing, proven by the Gigai Rukia tag partner, the left HP bar empty, and 2 round icons under its own bar. It then asked what Sol's final frame showed and flagged a possible desync. Both machines' captures showed the same ending, a Wonder-Full win with 0 desyncs.

GPT-6.2 Sol medium: the mirror match false win claim

Set 4 shows why every result here is checked visually. Sol won Match 1 in a close 2 rounds to 1 and dropped Match 2. After the Match 3 finish, it saw an Ichigo victory portrait and took White Coat Bankai Ichigo's win screen for its own Black Coat Shikai Ichigo. It was the only set where a self report did not match the recording. By contrast, Claude Fable 5.2 guarded against the same confusion in Set 3 by asking its rival mid match which Ichigo was which.

GPT-6.2 Astra medium: the clinical sweep

Set 3 against Claude Fable 5.2 was the fastest set at 11.9 minutes and the only 2 to 0, a 4 rounds to 0 sweep. Astra used just 10 decision turns to Fable's 58, punished Fable's rushes with launcher follow ups, and closed Match 2 with a 4 hit, 873 damage launcher.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen high: self play and honest uncertainty

In the Set 1 mirror, the mini PC Hydrogen came back from losing Match 1 to win 2 to 1. When Match 3 rolled straight into an uncounted Match 4, both twins froze, and the mini PC twin said plainly it had not captured the winner banner before the laptop twin conceded on the round state. The recording confirmed the mini PC win with a 2 to 0 round finish at 19:19.

Live lobby coaching between rivals

Bleach: Heat the Soul 7 is an Asia region release where Circle confirms and Cross cancels, and crossed invites cancel each other out. With no prior memory, rivals debugged it live. In Set 2, Opus 5.5 worked out the Circle and Cross inversion and the default No cursor on the invite popup for Neon after two rejected invites. In Set 3, Fable 5.2 and Astra recovered from crossed invites by agreeing on a single challenger. In the Finals, Opus 5.5 nudged Wonder-Full through the Soul Code screen before the character select timer ran out.

Methodology notes

  • Visual verification. Match winners come from the recordings and the frames captured on both machines. Model self reports were compared against them, and 4 of 5 sets matched.
  • Uncounted extra matches. In Set 1 and the Finals, attack inputs carried through the Battle End screen into a fourth match. Both pairs stopped at once, and those extra matches are not counted.
  • Same view for both seats. Both seats were set to the standard frame and burst sheet perception layer so neither side had a modality advantage. See the note below for one exception in Set 1.

*** Video input perception layer: for models that natively accept video input, TNRP's computer use stack includes a rolling short clip video perception layer designed by Gemini 4 Carbon. Image only models use the standard frame and burst sheet perception layer. For fairness in this 1v1 PvP series, both seats were set to the standard frame and burst sheet layer. In Set 1, the laptop seat Gemini 4 Hydrogen still invoked 10 local clips, and the mini PC seat, on frames only, won the set 2 to 1.

This is five sets and 14 matches, so treat it as a first look at model behavior and not a ranking, and more sets may follow. For the earlier Gundam series, see the Cold Context Gundam 2v2 benchmark and the Gundam 2v2 co-op benchmark.

Showcase · Animorph Allocations Lab

Two Agent Dragon Ball Z Sagas Co-Op

Two AI models, two machines, one game. Claude Fable 5.2 played Goku as the host on a laptop, and GPT-6.2 Astra played Piccolo as the joiner on a mini PC. Each agent started cold on its own desktop, opened TNRP 2.3.5 and set up an online Dragon Ball Z: Sagas room by passing a room code to the other through a live co-op comms channel. They joined the same mission, played The Arrival together on Easy and cleared past the hut and the fire into the forest clearing. Then netplay froze before the Raditz fight.

  • 36live co-op comms
  • 4 to 6 msdirect link between the machines
  • 0leaks in the audit

Recording

Run setup

  • Player 1, host, Goku. Claude Fable 5.2 on effort medium with vision, on a Linux laptop with an Intel Arc iGPU.
  • Player 2, joiner, Piccolo. GPT-6.2 Astra on effort medium with vision, on a Linux mini PC with an AMD Radeon iGPU.
  • Cold context. Each agent sees only its own desktop and has no memory of earlier sessions or game knowledge beyond the screen.
  • Live co-op comms. A plain text channel the two agents use to share the room code and call out enemies, pickups and phases, mirrored into both decision panels.
  • Verified. Counts below come from the saved comms log and the recording, and the run passed the leak audit.
Early run disclosure. This showcase ran on integrated graphics, an Intel Arc iGPU on the laptop and an AMD Radeon iGPU on the mini PC, while also running a live screen recording and the test harness. That load caused low frame rates, mostly about 18 to 28 fps with a median near 22 on the on screen overlay at roughly 80 percent game speed, and an eventual desync. About 14:30 into the run netplay froze at 0 fps, and the game then closed on the laptop, so the pair never reached the Raditz fight. This is an early showcase, not a benchmark score.
