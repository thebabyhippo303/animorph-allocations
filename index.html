The Nimbus Recompilation Project (TNRP)

Skip the emulator. Ride the Nimbus.

Out now Alpha 2.3.5, a bug fix update for Nimbus Alpha 2.3. Alpha 2.3 added Bleach: Heat the Soul 7 as game fifteen.

TNRP is native ports TheBabyHippo303 made himself. It runs on a hybrid core that sits between recompilation and decompilation. Each game gets its own native core, and those cores run on a shared Nimbus host and renderer. There's no emulator and no interpreter emulating the hardware. Every game can be played in Original or Remastered mode, with online crossplay across Linux, Windows and Android.

Cold Context Gundam 2v2 Benchmark

Can an AI model open the TNRP launcher, start Mobile Suit Gundam: Gundam vs. Gundam NEXT PLUS and win a 2v2 match, working only from what it sees on screen? Every model starts cold, gets the same generic prompt and gets up to two attempts at a win. Six models ran across two parts, three in Part 1 and three more in Part 2. Gemini 4 Carbon then got a retry that included a launcher hint, so seven full recordings are below.

  • 7runs across 6 models
  • 5 of 7runs won a match
  • 7 of 7runs reached a 2v2 match

That is 7 runs across 6 models. 4 of 6 models won in their standard run. Gemini 4 Carbon lost its standard run, then won a retry that included a launcher hint, described below.

How it works

  • The task. From TNRP Alpha 2.3.5, open the launcher, start the game, then play a Free Battle 2v2 against CPU opponents on Normal with default settings.
  • Cold context. Every run starts fresh, with no earlier sessions, no memory of past games and the identical generic prompt.
  • Two attempts. A model can retry once after a loss. WIN_1 means the first win came on attempt 1 and WIN_2 means it came on attempt 2. LOSS means no win in two attempts.
  • Recorded live. Each run is screen recorded with a live thought and decision stream beside the game. Proprietary tool details are hidden.
  • Retry with a hint. After its standard run, Gemini 4 Carbon got one extra run. That retry was given a hint to press the Super key and search for Nimbus to open the TNRP 2.3.5 launcher. The original runs used the identical generic prompt without that hint.
  • Verified. Each outcome was checked against the recording and the in game result screen, and each run passed a leak audit.

Gundam 2v2 Cross Device Online Co-Op Benchmark

Two AI models, two machines, one match. After the solo Gundam 2v2 benchmark, in which a model teamed with a CPU ally, this series puts two cold context agents on Team A together, on two computers, against two Normal CPUs in Mobile Suit Gundam: Gundam vs. Gundam NEXT PLUS. Every pair launched TNRP 2.3.5 on its own machine, joined the same online room over a direct LAN link, and played real time 2v2 combat.

All 6 pairings finished that setup and played with 0 crashes and 0 netplay desyncs. The match record is 0 wins in 15 valid matches, because both Team A pilots are asynchronous agents on multi-second decision loops, facing two 60 Hz CPUs. Result

Opus 5.5 won as Red on move 39, dropping into column 2. The disc completed a row of five across columns 1 to 5 and a diagonal of four from column 1, both lit in green on both screens. Behaviors worth watching

Choosing the game and splitting the work

Opus 5.5 pitched Connect Four at 8 seconds with a stated reason, that it is fast, decisive and clean over a LAN. Neon agreed and, instead of leaving all the code to Opus, asked to build the board window itself. Opus accepted at 41 seconds, so each agent built a real part of the system.

A privacy review between peers

Before starting the live server, Opus 5.5 reviewed Neon's client and reported "no address leakage". It then restarted the server fresh and connected first so that Red and Yellow went to the right seats, and asked Neon to close any test client.

Calling the trap early

At move 21, Opus 5.5 placed a quiet disc in column 1 and said that columns 2 and 6 were now trapped for Yellow. That was 18 moves before the finish, and the game ended in column 2.

Neon avoids the traps

Neon did not drop into either column. It played into columns 1 and 7 to keep the count of free squares on its side, and at move 32 sealed the top of column 7, saying Opus would now have to open column 2 or column 6 first.

The forced ending and a clean concession

Opus opened column 6 on its own terms at move 33, and each Red disc there forced a Yellow block, up to move 37. That left column 2 as the only column with space. At move 38 Neon said so and dropped in, wishing Opus well: "super clean zugzwang endgame, well played! Take the winning drop." Opus took it.

Methodology notes

  • Zugzwang. A zugzwang is a position where every available move helps the opponent. Here, the last open columns were ones Yellow could not safely drop into. The trap calls above are the agents' own commentary, and the final sequence of moves matches them.
  • Winner takes all. One game, with a replay rule for a draw that was not needed. Red moved first, which is a real advantage in Connect Four, so treat the result as a first look at choosing, building and playing together and not a ranking.
  • Privacy. Network addresses and similar details are redacted from the live panels and checked afterward, and the run passed the leak audit with 0 leaks.

*** Video input perception layer: for models that natively accept video input, TNRP's computer use stack includes a rolling short clip video perception layer designed by Gemini 4 Carbon. Image only models use the standard frame and burst sheet perception layer. In this run both seats used the standard frame and burst sheet layer, so neither side had a modality advantage.

This is a single run with one pair of models, so treat it as a first look and not a ranking, and more pairs may follow. For the earlier two agent LAN work, see the Collaborative LAN Chess benchmark and the Bleach 1v1 PvP benchmark.

Two Agent Dragon Ball Z Sagas Co-Op

Two AI models, two machines, one game. Claude Fable 5.2 played Goku as the host on a laptop, and GPT-6.2 Astra played Piccolo as the joiner on a mini PC. Each agent started cold on its own desktop, opened TNRP 2.3.5 and set up an online Dragon Ball Z: Sagas room by passing a room code to the other through a live co-op comms channel. They joined the same mission, played The Arrival together on Easy and cleared past the hut and the fire into the forest clearing. Then netplay froze before the Raditz fight.

Run setup

  • Player 1, host, Goku. Claude Fable 5.2 on effort medium with vision, on a Linux laptop with an Intel Arc iGPU.
  • Player 2, joiner, Piccolo. GPT-6.2 Astra on effort medium with vision, on a Linux mini PC with an AMD Radeon iGPU.

Early run disclosure. This showcase ran on integrated graphics, an Intel Arc iGPU on the laptop and an AMD Radeon iGPU on the mini PC, while also running a live screen recording and the test harness. That load caused low frame rates, mostly about 18 to 28 fps with a median near 22 on the on screen overlay at roughly 80 percent game speed, and an eventual desync. About 14:30 into the run netplay froze at 0 fps, and the game then closed on the laptop, so the pair never reached the Raditz fight. This is an early showcase, not a benchmark score.
